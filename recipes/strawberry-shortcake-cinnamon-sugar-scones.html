Strawberry Shortcake with Cinnamon Sugar Scones

Whipped cream, local strawberries

← All recipes

Everyone makes shortcake with a plain biscuit, which is fine and forgettable. Mine is a cinnamon sugar scone: a little richer, a little crisper on top, with a crackly sugar crust that stands up to the juice from the berries. Ten years of baking went into figuring out that the difference between good and great shortcake is the biscuit, not the fruit.

That said, the fruit has to be local and in season. Supermarket strawberries in February will not make this dessert. Wait for June.

  • Serves8
  • Active time30 minutes
  • Total time1 hour

The three weeks in June when Massachusetts strawberries are small, dark red all the way through, and taste like candy.

Ingredients

Cinnamon sugar scones

  • 2 cups (250 g) all-purpose flour
  • 1/4 cup granulated sugar
  • 1 tablespoon baking powder
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/2 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 6 tablespoons cold unsalted butter, cut into small cubes
  • 3/4 cup cold heavy cream, plus 2 tablespoons for brushing
  • 1 large egg
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 2 tablespoons sugar mixed with 1 teaspoon cinnamon, for the tops

Strawberries and cream

  • 1 quart local strawberries, hulled and quartered
  • 3 tablespoons granulated sugar
  • 1 teaspoon lemon juice
  • 1 1/2 cups cold heavy cream
  • 2 tablespoons powdered sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon vanilla extract

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 400°F and line a sheet pan with parchment.
  2. Whisk the flour, sugar, baking powder, cinnamon, and salt in a large bowl. Add the cold butter and work it in with your fingertips until the mixture looks like coarse sand with some pea-sized pieces left. Keep everything cold.
  3. Whisk the cream, egg, and vanilla together and pour over the flour mixture. Stir with a fork just until it comes together into a shaggy dough. Turn out onto a floured surface and pat (don't roll) into a round about 1 inch thick.
  4. Cut into 8 wedges, or use a 2 1/2-inch cutter for rounds. Set on the pan, brush the tops with cream, and sprinkle generously with the cinnamon sugar. Bake 15 to 18 minutes until risen and golden. Cool on a rack.
  5. While the scones bake, toss the strawberries with the sugar and lemon juice and let them sit for 30 minutes until they're glossy and swimming in their own syrup.
  6. Whip the cream with the powdered sugar and vanilla to soft peaks. Don't take it to stiff; it should slump a little.
  7. Split each scone, spoon berries and syrup over the bottom half, pile on whipped cream, and set the top on at an angle. Serve right away.

Chef’s notes

  • Scones can be baked in the morning and served that night. Don't split them until you're ready to assemble.
  • Never refrigerate strawberries you plan to eat the same day. Cold kills their perfume.
  • For a plated dinner, I cut smaller rounds and serve one per person, with the cream piped and a few whole berries on top.
