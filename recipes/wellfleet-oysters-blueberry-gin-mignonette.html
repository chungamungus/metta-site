Wellfleet Oysters with Blueberry Gin Mignonette

Pink peppercorn, tarragon

← All recipes

This is the bite I'm known for. Wellfleet oysters are briny and clean, and a classic shallot mignonette is the right idea but the wrong color for a July table. Fresh blueberries crushed into champagne vinegar with a splash of juniper-forward gin turn the whole thing violet, fruity, and a little wild. Pink peppercorns and tarragon finish it. It tastes like a summer evening on the Cape.

At tasting menus I freeze the same mixture and scrape it into a granita so each oyster gets a spoonful of purple snow. Both versions are below.

  • ServesMakes about 2/3 cup, enough for 2 dozen oysters
  • Active time15 minutes
  • Total time45 minutes

Peak Maine and Massachusetts blueberry season is July into August, exactly when I want to be eating oysters outside.

Ingredients

Mignonette

  • 1/2 cup fresh blueberries, plus a few extra for garnish
  • 3 tablespoons champagne vinegar (or white wine vinegar)
  • 3 tablespoons London dry gin
  • 1 small shallot, minced as finely as you can
  • 1 teaspoon pink peppercorns, lightly crushed
  • 1 teaspoon fresh tarragon, finely chopped
  • 1/2 teaspoon sugar
  • Pinch of salt

Oysters

  • 24 Wellfleet oysters (or any cold-water East Coast oyster), scrubbed
  • Crushed ice or rock salt, for serving

Method

  1. Put the blueberries in a small bowl and crush about half of them with the back of a fork so they release their juice but leave some whole. Add the vinegar, gin, shallot, peppercorns, sugar, and salt. Stir, and let it sit at room temperature for at least 30 minutes so the shallot mellows and the color deepens.
  2. Stir in the tarragon just before serving. Taste; it should be sharp, fruity, and a little sweet.
  3. To shuck: hold each oyster cupped side down in a folded towel. Work the tip of an oyster knife into the hinge, twist until it pops, then slide the blade along the top shell to free the muscle. Remove the top shell, then slide the knife under the oyster to loosen it from the bottom shell, keeping every drop of liquor.
  4. Arrange on crushed ice or rock salt. Spoon about 1/2 teaspoon of mignonette over each oyster, and top with a single blueberry if you want to show off.

Chef’s notes

  • Granita version: double the recipe, pour it into a shallow metal pan, and freeze. Scrape with a fork every 30 minutes for 2 to 3 hours until it's fluffy purple ice. Spoon a little onto each oyster right before serving. Best with a dry stout alongside, which sounds strange and works beautifully.
  • The gin matters. Use something juniper-forward like Beefeater or Tanqueray. A soft, floral gin disappears.
  • Make the mignonette up to a day ahead; add the tarragon at the last minute so it stays green.
