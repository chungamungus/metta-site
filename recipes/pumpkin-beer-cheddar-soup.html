Pumpkin Beer & Cheddar Soup

Vermont cheddar, pumpkin ale, chives

← All recipes

Beer cheese soup is a Wisconsin thing. This is the New England version: a good pumpkin ale, real pumpkin, and a lot of sharp Vermont cheddar. Ten years of brewing taught me that the spice in a pumpkin beer (cinnamon, nutmeg, allspice) is exactly what a cheddar soup wants, so the beer does double duty as the liquid and the seasoning.

Pick a pumpkin ale you'd actually drink. A thin, sugary one makes a thin, sugary soup.

  • Serves6
  • Active time30 minutes
  • Total time50 minutes

The first cold weekend in October, when pumpkin beers are on the shelves and you're finally ready for one.

Ingredients

Soup

  • 4 tablespoons unsalted butter
  • 1 yellow onion, diced
  • 2 carrots, diced
  • 2 celery stalks, diced
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1/4 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 (12 oz) bottle pumpkin ale
  • 3 cups chicken or vegetable stock
  • 1 cup pumpkin purée (canned is fine, not pie filling)
  • 1 cup heavy cream
  • 12 oz sharp Vermont cheddar, grated (Cabot Seriously Sharp or Grafton 2-year)
  • 1 teaspoon Dijon mustard
  • 1/2 teaspoon smoked paprika
  • A few dashes Worcestershire
  • Pinch of freshly grated nutmeg
  • Kosher salt and black pepper

To finish

  • Chives, thinly sliced
  • Toasted pepitas
  • Good bread, for dunking

Method

  1. Melt the butter in a soup pot over medium heat. Add the onion, carrots, celery, and a pinch of salt and cook, stirring, until soft but not browned, about 8 minutes. Add the garlic and cook 1 minute more.
  2. Sprinkle in the flour and stir constantly for 2 minutes to cook out the raw taste. Pour in the beer slowly, whisking, and let it bubble and thicken for a minute.
  3. Add the stock and pumpkin purée. Bring to a simmer and cook, uncovered, for 20 minutes, until the vegetables are completely tender.
  4. Blend until silky, either with an immersion blender in the pot or in batches in a countertop blender (fill it no more than halfway with hot soup). Return to the pot over low heat and stir in the cream, Dijon, paprika, Worcestershire, and nutmeg.
  5. Take the pot off the heat. Add the cheddar a handful at a time, stirring until each addition melts before adding the next. Never let it boil once the cheese is in or it will break and go grainy. Season with salt and pepper.
  6. Ladle into warm bowls and top with chives and pepitas. Serve with bread and the rest of the six-pack.

Chef’s notes

  • Grate the cheese yourself. Pre-shredded cheese is coated in starch and won't melt smoothly.
  • Reheat gently over low heat, never boiling. It thickens overnight; loosen with a splash of stock.
  • For a vegetarian version, use vegetable stock and skip the Worcestershire (or use a vegan one).
