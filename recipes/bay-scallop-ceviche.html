Bay Scallop Ceviche

Blood orange, chili oil, cilantro

← All recipes

Bay scallops are the sweetest thing that comes out of New England water, and they need almost nothing done to them. This is the dish I open winter dinners with when I want the table to wake up: cold, bright, a little heat, and a whole lot of citrus at the moment blood oranges are at their best.

The only rule is dry-packed scallops. Wet-packed scallops are soaked in a phosphate solution that makes them weep and turn to mush in acid. If the label doesn't say dry, ask.

  • Serves4 as a starter
  • Active time20 minutes
  • Total time50 minutes

Bay scallop season runs November through March. Nantucket and Peconic bays are the ones to ask for.

Ingredients

Ceviche

  • 1 lb dry-packed bay scallops, side muscle removed
  • 1/2 cup fresh lime juice (4 to 5 limes)
  • 2 blood oranges, one juiced (about 1/4 cup) and one cut into segments
  • 1 small shallot, minced
  • 1 serrano chili, sliced paper-thin
  • 1/2 teaspoon kosher salt, plus flaky salt to finish
  • 1/3 cup cilantro leaves

Chili oil

  • 1/4 cup neutral oil
  • 1 tablespoon Aleppo pepper or gochugaru
  • 1 small garlic clove, smashed
  • Pinch of salt

Method

  1. Make the chili oil first so it has time to steep. Warm the oil, garlic, and pepper flakes in a small pan over low heat until the oil just starts to shimmer and smells toasty, about 2 minutes. Pull it off the heat, add a pinch of salt, and let it sit. Fish out the garlic before serving.
  2. Pat the scallops completely dry. Halve any that are bigger than a nickel so everything cures at the same rate.
  3. In a glass or stainless bowl, combine the scallops, lime juice, blood orange juice, shallot, and kosher salt. Toss, cover, and refrigerate for 25 to 30 minutes. You want the outside opaque and the very center still translucent. Bay scallops are small and cure fast, so don't walk away.
  4. Pour off about half the liquid. Fold in the blood orange segments and serrano.
  5. Divide among chilled bowls or a platter. Spoon the chili oil over the top, scatter the cilantro, and finish with flaky salt. Serve immediately.

Chef’s notes

  • Serve within an hour of adding the citrus. Ceviche that sits overnight is a different, much worse dish.
  • No blood oranges? A cara cara or a regular navel works. Add a squeeze of grapefruit for the bitterness you're missing.
  • This is a great first course for a beer pairing dinner. A dry, tart gose or a Belgian wit makes the scallops taste even sweeter.
