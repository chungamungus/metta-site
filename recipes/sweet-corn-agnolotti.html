Sweet Corn Agnolotti

Brown butter, chives, parmesan

← All recipes

Agnolotti del plin are the little pinched pasta pillows of Piedmont, and they're the best way I know to make a lot of people very quiet at a dinner table. In summer I fill them with a sweet corn and ricotta filling, then sauce them with brown butter loosened with a stock made from the corn cobs, so nothing about the corn goes to waste.

This is a project, but it's a fun one, and the agnolotti freeze perfectly. Make them on a Sunday and you have a dinner party main in your freezer.

  • Serves4 to 6 (about 60 agnolotti)
  • Active time1 hour 30 minutes
  • Total time2 hours 30 minutes

Three weeks in August when the corn at the farm stand was picked that morning. Don't make this with anything else.

Ingredients

Pasta dough

  • 300 g (2 cups) 00 flour
  • 2 large eggs plus 3 egg yolks
  • 1 teaspoon olive oil
  • Pinch of salt

Corn filling

  • 4 ears fresh corn, kernels cut off and cobs reserved
  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter
  • 1 shallot, minced
  • 2 tablespoons heavy cream
  • 1/2 cup whole-milk ricotta, drained
  • 1/2 cup finely grated parmesan
  • Kosher salt and white pepper

Sauce

  • The reserved corn cobs
  • 6 tablespoons unsalted butter
  • 2 tablespoons chives, thinly sliced
  • Parmesan, for grating
  • Flaky salt

Method

  1. Make the dough: mix the flour, eggs, yolks, oil, and salt into a shaggy mass, then knead 8 to 10 minutes until smooth. Wrap and rest at least 30 minutes.
  2. Make the corn stock: put the cobs in a pot, cover with 4 cups water, and simmer 25 minutes. Strain and reduce to about 1 cup. This is the secret to the sauce.
  3. Make the filling: melt the butter in a skillet over medium heat and cook the shallot until soft. Add the corn kernels and a pinch of salt and cook until tender and sweet, 5 to 6 minutes. Transfer to a blender with the cream and purée until smooth. Cool, then fold in the ricotta and parmesan. Season with salt and white pepper. Chill until firm, at least 30 minutes, then transfer to a piping bag or a zip bag with the corner snipped.
  4. Roll a piece of dough through the pasta machine to the second-thinnest setting, into a long sheet about 5 inches wide. Lay it on a floured board. Pipe a line of filling about 1/2 inch thick along the length of the sheet, 1 inch from the bottom edge.
  5. Fold the bottom edge up and over the filling and press to seal, pushing out air. Then, with your thumb and forefinger, pinch the filled tube at 1-inch intervals to create little pillows (the "plin" is the pinch). Run a fluted pastry wheel along the top edge to trim, then cut between each pinch. Set the agnolotti on a floured sheet pan. Repeat with the remaining dough.
  6. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil. In a wide skillet, cook the butter over medium heat, swirling, until the milk solids turn nut-brown and it smells like toasted hazelnuts, 4 to 5 minutes. Add the corn stock and swirl; it will bubble and come together.
  7. Boil the agnolotti until they float and are tender, about 3 minutes. Lift them into the skillet with a slotted spoon, add a splash of pasta water, and toss until the sauce is glossy and clings. Plate, and finish with chives, grated parmesan, and flaky salt.

Chef’s notes

  • To freeze: lay the uncooked agnolotti on a floured sheet pan, freeze until solid, then bag them. Cook straight from frozen; add a minute.
  • If your ricotta is wet, drain it in a sieve lined with a coffee filter for an hour first, or the filling will leak.
  • For a dinner party I serve 8 to 10 per person as a pasta course. Blistered cherry tomatoes on top in late August are not a mistake.
