Midnight Fazzoletti

Calabrian chili, colatura, garlic

← All recipes

"Midnight pasta" is the Italian answer to the late-night fridge raid: garlic, anchovy, chili, and olive oil, made in the time it takes water to boil. My version uses fazzoletti, big handkerchief-shaped sheets of fresh pasta that catch the sauce in their folds, and colatura di alici, the amber Italian fish sauce that tastes like the ocean's best day.

It sounds aggressive on paper. On the plate it's savory, warm, and deeply comforting. This is the dish I make for guests who tell me they don't like anchovies.

  • Serves4
  • Active time30 minutes
  • Total time30 minutes (1 hour with fresh pasta)

Pantry cooking for the dead of March, when nothing local is growing yet and you want something with a pulse.

Ingredients

Fresh pasta (or use 12 oz dried pappardelle)

  • 300 g (2 cups) 00 flour, plus more for dusting
  • 3 large eggs
  • 1 teaspoon olive oil
  • Pinch of salt

Sauce

  • 1/3 cup good olive oil
  • 6 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
  • 6 oil-packed anchovy fillets
  • 1 to 2 tablespoons Calabrian chili paste (or 4 jarred Calabrian chilies, chopped)
  • 2 tablespoons capers, drained
  • 1 tablespoon colatura di alici
  • Zest of 1 lemon
  • 1/2 cup flat-leaf parsley, chopped
  • 1/2 cup panko toasted in 1 tablespoon olive oil until golden
  • Pecorino Romano, for grating

Method

  1. For the pasta: mound the flour on a board, make a well, and add the eggs, oil, and salt. Beat the eggs with a fork, gradually pulling in flour until a shaggy dough forms, then knead for 8 to 10 minutes until smooth and springy. Wrap and rest 30 minutes.
  2. Roll the dough through a pasta machine down to the second-thinnest setting. Cut the sheets into rough 4-inch squares. Dust with flour and lay them out in a single layer.
  3. Bring a large pot of water to a boil and salt it well. Set a large skillet over medium-low heat with the olive oil and garlic. Cook slowly until the garlic is golden at the edges and the oil is fragrant, 3 to 4 minutes. Don't rush this; burnt garlic is the only way to ruin this dish.
  4. Add the anchovies and stir until they dissolve into the oil, about 1 minute. Add the chili paste and capers and cook 30 seconds more. Turn off the heat.
  5. Cook the fazzoletti until just tender, 2 to 3 minutes for fresh (or per the package for dried). Scoop out a cup of pasta water before draining.
  6. Return the skillet to medium heat, add the pasta and 1/2 cup of the pasta water, and toss until the sauce clings and looks glossy, adding more water as needed. Off the heat, add the colatura, lemon zest, and parsley and toss again.
  7. Plate, and top with the toasted breadcrumbs and a light grating of pecorino.

Chef’s notes

  • Colatura is very salty. Taste before you add any salt to the sauce; between the anchovies, capers, colatura, and pecorino, you probably won't need it.
  • Calabrian chili paste is worth seeking out. It's fruity and smoky as well as hot. Red pepper flakes work in a pinch, but start with 1/2 teaspoon.
  • Pairing: a crisp Italian pilsner, or a bone-dry Vermentino.
