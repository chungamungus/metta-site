Cider-Brined Pork Chops

Grilled apples, mustard greens, cider jus

← All recipes

Pork chops are a sad, gray main course in most homes because they're cooked like steak and they're not steak. Two things fix that: a brine, which seasons the chop through and keeps it juicy, and a low-and-slow reverse sear, which cooks it evenly with a deep crust and a rosy center. Fresh apple cider in the brine makes the pork taste faintly of orchard, and grilled apples on the side finish the thought.

Ask your butcher for double-cut, bone-in chops from the rib end. Thin chops can't be saved by any technique.

  • Serves4
  • Active time40 minutes
  • Total time1 hour 30 minutes, plus 6 to 12 hours brining

Apple season. Buy the cider from the same orchard as the apples.

Ingredients

Brine and chops

  • 2 cups fresh apple cider
  • 2 cups water
  • 1/4 cup Diamond Crystal kosher salt (use 2 1/2 tablespoons if using Morton)
  • 3 tablespoons dark brown sugar
  • 1 tablespoon black peppercorns
  • 1 teaspoon yellow mustard seeds
  • 2 bay leaves and 4 sprigs thyme
  • 4 bone-in double-cut pork chops, about 1 1/2 inches thick (12 to 14 oz each)
  • 2 tablespoons neutral oil
  • 3 tablespoons butter, 3 garlic cloves, and a few thyme sprigs, for basting

Apples and greens

  • 2 firm apples (Honeycrisp, Mutsu, or Northern Spy), cored and cut into 8 wedges each
  • 1 tablespoon butter
  • 1 bunch mustard greens, stemmed and torn
  • 1 garlic clove, sliced
  • 1 tablespoon cider vinegar

Cider jus

  • 1 cup fresh apple cider
  • 1 cup chicken stock
  • 1 teaspoon Dijon mustard
  • 2 tablespoons cold butter

Method

  1. Make the brine: bring 1 cup of the water to a boil with the salt, sugar, peppercorns, mustard seeds, bay, and thyme, stirring to dissolve. Add the remaining cold water and the cider and cool completely. Submerge the chops and refrigerate 6 to 12 hours. Longer than 12 makes the texture spongy.
  2. Remove the chops, rinse briefly, and pat very dry. Let them sit at room temperature for 30 minutes while you heat the oven to 250°F.
  3. Set the chops on a rack over a sheet pan and roast until the center reads 115°F on an instant-read thermometer, 30 to 40 minutes depending on thickness.
  4. Heat the oil in a large cast-iron skillet over high heat until it smokes. Sear the chops 2 minutes per side until deeply browned. Add the butter, garlic, and thyme, tilt the pan, and baste the chops with the foaming butter for a minute. Pull them at 138 to 140°F and rest on a board for 10 minutes; they'll carry over to 145°F.
  5. While the chops rest, make the jus in the same skillet: pour off the fat, add the cider and stock, and scrape up the browned bits. Boil until reduced to about 1/2 cup, then whisk in the Dijon and cold butter off the heat. Season to taste.
  6. In a separate skillet (or on the grill, if it's still warm out), cook the apple wedges in the butter over medium-high heat until caramelized on both sides and just tender, 5 to 6 minutes. Set aside. In the same pan, wilt the mustard greens with the garlic for 2 minutes, then splash with the vinegar and season.
  7. Plate each chop over the greens with apples alongside, and spoon the jus over the top.

Chef’s notes

  • Salt matters. Diamond Crystal and Morton kosher salt are not interchangeable by volume. Morton is nearly twice as salty per tablespoon.
  • Cooked to 140 to 145°F, the center will be faintly pink. That's correct and safe; today's pork is not the pork of the 1980s.
  • A dry New England hard cider is the pairing. So is an Oktoberfest lager.
