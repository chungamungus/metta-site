Maple Creemee with Homemade Waffle Cones

Vermont maple, toasted walnuts

← All recipes

A creemee is Vermont's name for soft serve, and a maple creemee from a roadside stand in March is one of the great pleasures of living in New England. Mine is a proper French custard base made with dark maple syrup, served straight from the churn while it's still soft, in a waffle cone you made an hour ago. The cone alone is worth the price of a waffle cone iron.

The maple does something useful here: its sugar and water content keep the ice cream softer than a standard base, which is exactly what you want in a creemee.

  • ServesAbout 1 1/2 quarts, and 8 cones
  • Active time1 hour
  • Total timeOvernight chill, then 30 minutes

Sugaring season. Late February through early April, when the sap runs and the roadside stands open.

Ingredients

Maple creemee

  • 2 cups heavy cream
  • 1 cup whole milk
  • 3/4 cup Grade A Dark Robust maple syrup (the darker the better)
  • 5 large egg yolks
  • 1/4 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract

Waffle cones

  • 2 large egg whites
  • 1/2 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon kosher salt
  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter, melted and cooled
  • 1/4 cup whole milk
  • 1/2 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 2/3 cup (85 g) all-purpose flour

To finish

  • 1/2 cup walnuts, toasted and chopped
  • A drizzle of maple syrup

Method

  1. Make the base: warm the cream, milk, and maple syrup in a saucepan over medium heat until steaming. In a bowl, whisk the yolks, sugar, and salt until pale. Slowly whisk a cup of the hot cream into the yolks to temper them, then pour everything back into the pan.
  2. Cook over medium-low heat, stirring constantly with a spatula and scraping the bottom, until the custard thickens enough to coat the spatula and reads 170 to 175°F, 5 to 8 minutes. Don't let it boil.
  3. Strain into a bowl, stir in the vanilla, and chill completely, ideally overnight. A cold base churns into a smoother ice cream.
  4. Make the cone batter: whisk the egg whites, sugar, and salt until frothy. Whisk in the butter, milk, and vanilla, then the flour until smooth. Rest 15 minutes.
  5. Heat a waffle cone iron (or a nonstick skillet over medium heat). Spoon 3 tablespoons of batter into the center and cook until deep golden, 1 to 2 minutes per side in a skillet, spreading it into a thin 6-inch circle with the back of a spoon. Working fast, wrap the hot wafer around a cone form (or a rolled cardboard cone wrapped in foil), pinch the point closed, and hold for 20 seconds until set. Cool on a rack. Repeat.
  6. Churn the base in an ice cream maker according to the manufacturer's directions until it's the texture of soft serve, 20 to 25 minutes.
  7. Serve immediately, straight from the churn, swirled into the cones with toasted walnuts and a drizzle of maple over the top. (Any leftover base can be frozen; it firms up into an excellent hard maple ice cream.)

Chef’s notes

  • Grade A Dark Robust (the old Grade B) has far more maple flavor than Golden or Amber. Don't use the light stuff here.
  • Cones can be made a day ahead and kept in an airtight container. If they soften, crisp them in a 300°F oven for 3 minutes.
  • For a plated dinner I serve the creemee in a small glass with the cone standing up in it, so no one has to eat a cone in a nice shirt.
