Meyer Lemon Olive Oil Cake

Crème fraîche, honey, sea salt

← All recipes

If I could only make one dessert for the rest of my career, it would be this cake. It's plush, fragrant, a little savory from the olive oil, and it keeps for days. Meyer lemons, which are a cross between a lemon and a mandarin, give it a floral sweetness that regular lemons can't.

Use an olive oil you'd want to taste on bread. It's the main flavor. A peppery Tuscan oil makes a slightly grown-up cake; a buttery Ligurian one makes a gentler one. Both are right.

  • ServesOne 9-inch cake, 10 slices
  • Active time20 minutes
  • Total time1 hour 30 minutes

Meyer lemons show up in New England markets from December through February, right when we need them most.

Ingredients

Cake

  • 3 large eggs, room temperature
  • 1 1/4 cups (250 g) granulated sugar
  • Zest of 3 Meyer lemons
  • 1 cup (215 g) extra-virgin olive oil
  • 3/4 cup whole milk, room temperature
  • 1/4 cup fresh Meyer lemon juice
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 2 cups (250 g) all-purpose flour
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking soda
  • 3/4 teaspoon kosher salt

Glaze and serving

  • 1 cup powdered sugar
  • 2 to 3 tablespoons Meyer lemon juice
  • Crème fraîche, honey, and flaky sea salt, to serve

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 350°F. Grease a 9-inch springform pan, line the bottom with parchment, and grease the parchment.
  2. In a large bowl, rub the lemon zest into the sugar with your fingers until it's damp and fragrant. Add the eggs and whisk vigorously for a full minute until pale and slightly thickened.
  3. Whisk in the olive oil in a slow stream, then the milk, lemon juice, and vanilla. The batter may look a little broken; that's fine.
  4. In a separate bowl, whisk together the flour, baking powder, baking soda, and salt. Add to the wet ingredients and fold gently with a spatula just until no dry streaks remain. Don't overmix.
  5. Pour into the pan and bake for 45 to 50 minutes, until the top is golden, the edges pull from the pan, and a skewer in the center comes out with a few moist crumbs. Cool in the pan for 15 minutes, then release the ring and cool completely.
  6. Whisk the powdered sugar with enough lemon juice to make a thick but pourable glaze. Pour it over the cooled cake and let it set for 20 minutes.
  7. Serve each slice with a spoonful of crème fraîche, a thread of honey, and a few flakes of sea salt.

Chef’s notes

  • No Meyer lemons? Use regular lemons but cut the juice to 3 tablespoons and add 1 tablespoon of orange juice.
  • This cake is better on day two. Keep it covered at room temperature for up to 4 days.
  • For a dinner party, bake it the morning of and glaze it after the main course goes out.
